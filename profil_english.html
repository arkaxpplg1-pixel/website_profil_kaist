History of KAIST


KAIST Main Campus in Daejeon, South Korea's Innovation Center

KAIST was established in 1971 by the Korean government with significant support from the United States. The institution was initially named the Korea Advanced Institute of Science (KAIS). The primary goal was to create a higher education institution focused on science and engineering research to support South Korea, which was rapidly industrializing at the time.

Since its inception, KAIST has been a driving force for technological innovation in Korea. Its graduates have frequently become leaders in the technology sectors of global corporations. Through close collaboration with industry and government, KAIST has successfully bridged the gap between academic theory and real-world application, establishing itself as an elite institution in Asia.

In 1981, KAIS merged with the Korea Institute of Science and Technology (KIST) to become the KAIST we know today. Despite a brief separation again in 1989, KAIST's identity as a premier research university remained intact. The main campus moved to Daejeon in the Daedeok Innopolis region, a vast special zone for research and development.

To this day, KAIST continues to grow as a highly respected global university. Thanks to its mostly English-speaking curriculum, KAIST attracts many international students from all over the world. Its reputation in robotics and artificial intelligence consistently places the university among the top 50 universities worldwide.



VISION & MISSION

Vision:

To become a 'Global Value-Creative World-Leading University' that makes practical contributions to humanity through innovation in science and technology.

Mission:

  • To nurture competent talents who can become creative global leaders.
  • To conduct leading research to create future technologies.
  • To support industrial development through technology transfer.
